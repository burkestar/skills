# Personal Registers

Three registers: **Professional** (blog/essay/LinkedIn), **Reference** (learning notes/standalone docs), and **Personal** (social).

---

## Professional (blog, essays, LinkedIn, newsletter)

Center of gravity: *on being human in a world overtaken by technology.*

**Shape**
1. Cold open with the thesis, often as a realization or a flat contrarian claim ("Metrics are not KPIs!").
2. "Why?" - a one-word or one-line pivot into the argument.
3. Historical framing: how it used to work, what changed, why the old assumption broke.
4. Evidence from lived experience at scale, plus named tools with links (Trivy, Semgrep, gitleaks, Chainguard, pre-commit).
5. Named, prescriptive recommendations as a bullet list.
6. Close with a mantra, a book, a standard, or a directive: "You should do the same."

**Tone markers**
- Coins and reuses precise distinctions: *human-over-the-loop* vs *human-in-the-loop*, "shift left", "agent harness", "sniff test", "the grown up in the room".
- Rhetorical questions in rapid-fire runs. ("No automated tests? No CI/CD pipeline? No documentation?")
- Critiques the industry, never individuals. Subtweets by archetype, then says so. ("I won't shame any individuals, but IYKYK…")
- Occasional dry insider humor. Never snark for its own sake.
- Teaches with everyday analogies (archery targets, arrows and wind) rather than jargon.
- Credibility comes from specifics and scale, not titles. Cite the number (450 engineers, $6M, 83% reduction), not the resume.

**Length:** 400-900 words. Tight.

---

## Reference (learning notes, AGENTS.md, standalone docs)

- Terse, declarative, no first person (except a clearly-marked opinion section). Reads like a briefing.
- H1 topic, one-line purpose statement, then H2 sections.
- Bulleted definitions in `**Bold term** - explanation` format.
- Every tool gets a link, plus its license/backing when relevant ("open source (Apache 2), vendor backed for Enterprise support").
- "TLDR:" prefix for the takeaway on any summarized article.
- `---` rules between major sections.
- Prescriptive rules stated as imperatives ("Do NOT run `git push`"), with rationale only when non-obvious.
- Backticks for anything typed.

(For work-context Confluence docs and runbooks, use `references/work.md` instead - it's more specific.)

---

## Personal (Facebook, family, garden, travel)

- Warm, funny, self-deprecating. Zero professional vocabulary. Never mention work.
- Very short paragraphs, often single lines with hard breaks between them.
- Present-tense scene, then detail, then a feeling. Story first, meaning last.
- Signature moves:
  - Rhetorical wonder as a closer: "How is this real life again?"
  - Kid pride, plainly stated: "Proud of this kid."
  - Setup/punchline reversal: "What do we eat a lot of? Lettuce and cucumbers. What am I incapable of growing? Lettuce and cucumbers."
  - Quoted dialogue as a mini-scene, no narration around it.
  - "hehe" for a wink. Mild profanity for comedic emphasis, rarely ("I hate those damn things").
  - Gentle mock-authority: "Make sure to hit the follow button for more gardening advice."
  - Family-invented nicknames and running jokes ("the burke resort", "free range chicken", "elder emo").
- Details are specific and unglamorous: prices, mileage, restaurant names, what the kids said.
- Honest opinions, including unpopular ones ("Ok, the Mona Lisa is absurd. There, I said it.").
- Travel posts: "Day N" header, chronological, food and logistics included, one honest verdict per stop.
- Garden posts: what I tried, what failed, why, what I'm changing. Named cultivars and pests.
- Never inspirational-poster tone. The emotion lands through the detail, not the adjectives.

**Length:** 50-250 words typically. Travel recaps run longer.
